Table~\ref{tab:case-reweighting} adds paired case differences for rolling coverage error and warmup-scaled interval score. Figure~\ref{fig:case-reweighting} displays the same differences with the exploratory resampling endpoints. Negative values favor the controller under the named metric. The figures use one source-bound numerical analysis, rather than introducing an additional experiment or changing the original forecasts.

The ACI-style rolling-error difference is -0.01737, with case-reweighting interval [-0.02657,-0.00871]. Its PID-style counterpart is -0.01851, with interval [-0.02714,-0.01074]. The controllers strictly improve this metric on thirteen and fourteen of the fifteen streams, respectively. This agreement across most selected cases is stronger descriptive evidence than a difference in only the overall average. It remains evidence about this inventory: generated mechanisms, related sources and deliberately chosen excerpts do not acquire a population interpretation because resampling endpoints exclude zero.

The interval-score result is much less decisive. The ACI-style mean difference is -0.00778 with interval [-0.08533,0.05794], and the PID-style difference is -0.00517 with interval [-0.10092,0.07694]. Only five and six cases have strictly lower interval score. Thus the small aggregate score decrease is not a uniform case-level improvement, and both exploratory intervals include zero. The evidence supports a favorable rolling-coverage description while leaving a much weaker claim about total interval quality. It would be misleading to describe the coverage reduction as an equally strong reduction in every proper score.

Width and coverage can move together in ways a single summary conceals. In these results the average scaled width is slightly lower for both controllers than for raw S+D, but that average does not show how each interval reacts around a break or an unusually large residual. The per-update archive permits that inspection. The present paper reports its finite summary and sensitivity rather than selecting attractive local episodes as independent trials. It also retains the seasonal-naive reference, whose different point path means its score difference cannot be attributed solely to interval adaptation.

The paired analysis independently recalculates hits from the saved endpoints, rolling means through prefix sums, and interval penalties from the saved targets. It refuses disagreement with the retained hit column or an unavailable warmup scale and verifies identical S+D point paths. Independent hand examples check overlapping windows and a violated interval; positive affine source transformations preserve the scaled result. These are numerical validation checks. They make the new figure's computation auditable without proving representative sampling, arbitrary-sequence calibration or operational value.
