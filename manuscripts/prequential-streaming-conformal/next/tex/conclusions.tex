\section{Conclusions}
On fifteen selected streams at nominal eighty-percent coverage, the historical ACI-style and PID-style buffer controllers lower mean rolling absolute coverage error from .0830 to .0656 and .0645. The reduction is smaller than the ratio obtained by averaging signed deviations before taking their absolute value. Reconstructed per-update records, explicit score definitions and a deterministic inequality show why those statistics answer different questions.

Retrospective joint case resampling gives rolling-error differences of -.01737 and -.01851, with exploratory intervals excluding zero for this case-weighting exercise. The corresponding scaled interval-score differences are close to zero, their intervals include zero, and most cases do not improve that score. This limits the empirical conclusion to the specified coverage behavior rather than a general advantage in probabilistic prediction.

The replay preserves its exact bases, numerical floors, clipping and inherited gains. It does not evaluate native state updates or faithfully reproduce published Conformal PID Control. Completing those comparisons, independent reader review and prospectively designed authentic-data studies are future requirements. The current working revision documents a reproducible finite evaluation and its limits; it is not a new coverage guarantee or an accepted deployment policy.
