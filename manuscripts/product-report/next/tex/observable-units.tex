\subsection{Published numerical providers and observable units}
\label{sec:observable-units}
The executable numerical provider is ForecastScope 0.1.4, distributed independently of the application. Eleven diagnostic families, the finite-prefix forecasting kernels, ContextMemory and the temporally validated combination procedures resolve to fourteen installed provider modules. Product-specific source admission, model-runtime policies, case definitions and artifact exports remain path-invoked application tooling. The distinction separates a reusable numerical implementation from the experiment that selected its inputs, settings and reserved targets. A procedure inventory identifies each called provider, distribution version and source digest. The original source maps attached to retained studies resolve to immutable source archives; a matching current function name does not change the identity of an earlier experiment.

Let \(U_y\) denote the response unit and \(U_x\) a covariate unit. A positive change of response units has the form \(y'_t=a y_t+b\), with \(a>0\). Forecast locations should transform by the same map, widths and absolute errors by \(a\), and dimensionless quantities such as MASE and coverage should remain unchanged where their denominators are defined. WQL is invariant under positive multiplication; its absolute-target denominator does not generally make it invariant under translation. These are numerical consistency properties, not accuracy or calibration theorems. They apply within finite, representable arithmetic and the supported method assumptions. They cannot compensate for measurement error, a changed observation cadence or an incorrectly specified seasonal lag.

The replay ridge adapter estimates location and scale from the complete prefix already available at its forecast origin. It standardizes the response and, when used, each historical covariate before solving its penalized problem:
\begin{align}
z_t&=(y_t-\mu_y)/s_y,\qquad x_t=(c_t-\mu_c)/s_c,\\
\widehat\beta&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\beta}
\sum_{t=\ell}^{n-1}\left(z_t-\beta_0-\beta_1z_{t-1}-\beta_mz_{t-m}-\beta_cx_t\right)^2
+\lambda\left(\beta_1^2+\beta_m^2+\beta_c^2\right).
\end{align}
Here \(\ell=\max(1,m)\); \(\mu_y,\mu_c\) and \(s_y,s_c\) are the observed-prefix means and population standard deviations. The fixed penalty is \(\lambda=1\), and the intercept is unpenalized. The blind adapter omits the covariate term. A constant historical covariate contributes a zero standardized column: an effect cannot be estimated from absent historical covariate variation. Future covariate values enter only the forecast design rows, using historical location and scale. They never determine the fitting transformation. The covariate-aware call requires both historical and available future covariates. The penalty acts on dimensionless coefficients, rather than choosing response or covariate measurement units implicitly. This is the current adapter's formulation; historical replay experiments retain their original raw-coordinate ridge implementation and recorded source identity.

Recursive predictions are transformed back by \(\widehat y=\mu_y+s_y\widehat z\). The residual standard deviation used in the Gaussian widening rule is likewise mapped back with \(s_y\). A zero residual standard deviation remains zero; it is not replaced with a fixed positive quantity in unknown response units. These residual intervals retain their distributional assumptions and are not conformal coverage guarantees. An exactly constant response prefix is identified by its observed range, rather than by requiring a floating-point variance calculation to equal zero. The local neural adaptation and ridge adapter then return its observed level. This deterministic boundary behavior supplies no evidence about the width appropriate for a stochastic future following that prefix.

Degenerate diagnostic denominators also have explicit meanings. Decomposition strengths use the actual residual and component variances; a zero denominator gives strength zero under the implemented convention, while a zero numerator is preserved. Cross-correlation is unavailable when either centered input has zero variation. A zero median absolute deviation yields zero screening score for exact median ties and an unbounded screening score for departures, without adding an arbitrary measured offset. Context-memory origins whose fitting prefix has no positive loss scale are excluded from normalized competence estimation; an empty admitted memory receives equal expert allocation. These screening and allocation conventions describe undefined-information cases. They do not establish that a constant process has a predictable future or that every departure is an erroneous observation.

Scientific transports preserve floating-point response values, predictions, covariates and raw-unit errors. CSV exports use round-trip float conversion; newly generated trace arrays and raw series descriptors are not quantized to a fixed number of decimal places. Reader-facing formatting is separate. The retained historical traces keep their original four-decimal representation and the source-specific reconstruction tolerance described above. Tests apply small and large positive unit factors, independent covariate conversions and location shifts; they separately exercise constant-prefix and sparse-deviation cases. Installed-provider comparisons on the fifteen selected histories preserve the ordinary-scale forecast paths, expert allocations and admitted origins. That finite compatibility result does not turn the unit tests into a new forecasting benchmark or replace the source identity of the retained studies.
